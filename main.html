Projects

2025–2026Project Lead

Nighttime Global Ocean Chlorophyll Prediction Using Deep Learning and Day–Night Alternating Training

National Undergraduate Innovation and Entrepreneurship Training Program

This project addresses the difficulty of obtaining nighttime ocean chlorophyll data through conventional satellite remote sensing. It integrates multi-source data from GOCI2, HYCOM, and ERA5 and develops two deep-learning models, LSTM and Spatio-Temporal Transformer. A day–night alternating training strategy feeds nighttime observations into iterative training, enabling the models to predict nighttime chlorophyll concentrations and overcoming the limitations of traditional remote-sensing methods.

2025–2026Project Lead

Environmental and Socioeconomic Determinants of the Global Prevalence and Mortality of Leishmaniasis Using Remote Sensing and GIS

Undergraduate Innovation Capacity Enhancement Project

To investigate the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of global cutaneous and mucocutaneous leishmaniasis, this project integrates Global Burden of Disease data, World Bank indicators, and remote-sensing environmental data. Moran’s I and Getis-Ord Gi* are used to identify spatial clusters and hotspot migration, while Spearman correlation, OLS, GWR, and GTWR models quantify the effects of environmental and socioeconomic factors on disease prevalence and mortality, supporting global risk identification and prevention.

2024–2025Project Lead

MetEva-Based Evaluation of the Impact of FY-4A GIIRS Data Assimilation on Severe-Weather Forecasting and Its Teaching Application

University-Level Undergraduate Research Project

This project evaluates the performance of the CMA-MESO mesoscale numerical prediction system in Zhejiang Province, with emphasis on how FY-4A GIIRS satellite data assimilation affects forecasts of temperature, wind speed, precipitation, Meiyu-season rainstorms, and typhoons. After MetEva-standard station-data normalization and data matching through bilinear and Cressman interpolation, forecasts are quantitatively evaluated using MAE, RMSE, R², and threat-score metrics.
